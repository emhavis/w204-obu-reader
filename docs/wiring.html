Before you power up

  1. Bench first. Get the reader talking to the simulator (Figure 1) before you plug anything into the car.
  2. Termination. Most SN65HVD230 boards have a 120 Ω resistor marked 121. Bench: keep it on both boards. Car: lift or cut it on the reader's board. Check with a meter: about 60 Ω across CAN-H/CAN-L on the bench, and about 60 Ω at the car's OBD pins 6–14 with the ignition off.
  3. Test the power section on its own. Feed it 12–14 V from a bench supply or the car with no ESP32 connected. Check for 5.0 V on the LM2596's USB output before you plug the ESP32 in.
  4. One power source at a time. In the car the ESP32's USB port is taken by the LM2596 cable. To watch serial logs in the car, unplug that cable and use the laptop's USB instead, with the laptop on battery.
  5. Boot pins. GPIO2 (TFT DC) and GPIO15 (TFT CS) are ESP32 boot-strap pins. They work with the TFT attached, but if flashing ever fails, unplug the TFT and retry. GPIO12 is deliberately unused, because pulling it high at boot breaks startup.
  6. Breadboard fit. The DevKitC V4 is wide. On one 830-point breadboard it leaves only one free hole per pin on one side. Put two breadboards side by side with the ESP32 across the gap, or use the female-to-male jumpers straight to the header.
  7. OBD cable colours vary by seller. Identify pins 4, 5, 6, 14 and 16 with a continuity test before you solder anything.
